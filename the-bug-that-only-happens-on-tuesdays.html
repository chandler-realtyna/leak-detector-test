The Bug That Only Happens on Tuesdays

Every team has one of these stories. The report that fails on Tuesdays. The job that double-runs every October. The invoice that's off by exactly one hour, but only for some customers.

Time-dependent bugs are unusually good at hiding because the systems that produce them are tested on whatever day the developer happened to be writing the test, and calendars are far weirder than the mental model most code carries around. Below is a short field guide to the recurring offenders.

Daylight saving as a silent duplicate-events generator

A scheduler that fires "every hour on the hour" using wall-clock time will either skip an hour or repeat one, twice a year, depending on hemisphere and jurisdiction. The repeat is the more dangerous of the two, because it looks like success — the job ran! — it just ran twice, and if the job isn't idempotent, that's two charges, two emails, or two rows in a table that expected one. The fix is almost always to schedule in UTC and only convert to local time for display, but plenty of cron-adjacent systems were configured against local time before anyone thought to check.

Week boundaries are not universal

ISO 8601 says the week starts on Monday. A large share of US-built software assumes Sunday. A report that buckets "this week" by day-of-week arithmetic will silently produce different results depending on which convention the library author picked, and it usually surfaces as a one-day-off discrepancy that someone notices only when comparing two systems side by side — often on, appropriately, a Monday morning.

The leap second nobody budgeted for

Leap seconds are rare enough that most systems never hit one in testing, which is exactly why they're dangerous. A monotonic clock assumption baked into a distributed lock or a rate limiter can produce a negative duration for one second, once, and whatever code path handles negative durations — usually none — gets exercised for the first time in production. Several major outages trace back to exactly this.

"Tuesday" is doing a lot of work in date math

Off-by-one errors in date arithmetic tend to cluster around specific weekdays because month lengths and week lengths don't divide evenly. A report generated as today - 7 days to get "last week's Tuesday" will occasionally land on a Monday or Wednesday around month boundaries if any part of the pipeline truncates to midnight in the wrong timezone first. The bug then reproduces reliably, but only once every few weeks, which is long enough for the original context to be forgotten by the time someone reopens the ticket.

What actually helps

Store and compute in UTC. Convert to local time exactly once, at the boundary where a human reads the value. Treat every date library's "add one day" as a DST-unsafe operation unless it explicitly says otherwise. And when a bug report says "only on Tuesdays" or "only in March," believe it immediately — vague as it sounds, it is usually the single most precise clue available, and the person reporting it has already done half your debugging for you.
